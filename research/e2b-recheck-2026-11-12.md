## Contradictions between first-party pages

1. **Enterprise minimum: $2,000 (rendered) vs $3,000 (Markdown twin) — the rendered page moved.** Rendered pricing estimator: "Enterprise starts at a $2,000/mo commit, 500 concurrent included." → https://e2b.dev/pricing. The Markdown twin: "A $3,000 monthly minimum applies to Enterprise usage." → https://e2b.dev/pricing.md. The repo's `check-rates` baseline (captured 2026-10-30) had `$3000` on the rendered page and now reports `$2000`, so this is a **change on the human page**, not a scraping artefact; the `.md` twin simply was not regenerated. **Which to model:** $2,000/mo commit, 500 concurrent included, and treat `pricing.md`'s $3,000 as stale. Note that "commit" and "minimum" may not be identical quantities (platform commit vs total spend), so the sentence should keep "commit" wording rather than silently becoming "minimum".
2. **Enterprise concurrency: 500 included vs "1,100+".** Rendered page: "500 concurrent included". `docs.e2b.dev/billing.md`: "| **Concurrent sandboxes …** | 10 | 100 - 1,100 … | 1,100+ |". **Which to model:** the docs table is a ceiling for the plan ladder; the pricing line is what the commit includes. Quote both, do not average.
3. **Default sandbox RAM: three figures.** Pricing page: "The default sandbox has 2 vCPUs and 4 GiB of RAM." Billing FAQ: "Start with default resources (2 vCPU, 512 MiB RAM)". Template build limits: "Rebuilding without them writes the defaults of 2 vCPU and 1024 MiB". **Which to model:** the site's existing caveat says two; it is now three. The build-limits page is the most specific about what a sandbox created from a rebuilt template actually gets (1 GiB), the billing FAQ about the cheap starting point (512 MiB), and the pricing page about the calculator's default shape (4 GiB). Costs in this guide use explicit shapes, so the contradiction affects only the "default" prose, not A/B/C.
4. **Pro concurrency add-ons: for sale vs "temporarily unavailable".** Pricing page and estimator sell Pro+ 600 at +$500/mo and Pro++ 1,100 at +$1,000/mo; the 12 October changelog says "Pro concurrency add-ons are temporarily unavailable to buy, so contact support instead." **Which to model:** the pricing page is the current offer (12 Nov); the changelog note is a month old and may be stale, but a buyer following the docs could be told to contact support.
5. **SSO/SCIM: exists vs planned.** `docs.e2b.dev/workspaces.md`: "Workspaces with SSO or SCIM have invites disabled. Members join through your identity provider instead." and the 9 Nov changelog treats "SSO and SCIM sign-ins" as unchanged. `e2b.dev/enterprise.md`: "SSO, SCIM, and RBAC are planned; ask E2B about timelines." **Which to model:** SSO/SCIM exist for at least some workspaces (docs describe their behaviour) but are not documented as a product, plan gate or setup path; the enterprise page is stale or refers to a fuller RBAC release. Verdict `partly`, contradiction preserved.
6. **BYOP: GA vs private beta.** `docs.e2b.dev/network/byop.md`: "BYOP is available on E2B Cloud and in BYOC environments." `e2b.dev/enterprise.md`: "Outbound traffic can be routed through a SOCKS5 proxy the customer runs (private beta)". **Which to model:** the docs page is the operational contract and is linked from internet-access; treat BYOP as available, note the enterprise page's beta label.
7. **BYOC on Azure.** `e2b.dev/enterprise.md` now says BYOC is available on "AWS, GCP, and Azure"; the same page's Embed section also documents a single-node Azure Terraform module. The site's profile/research previously left Azure unresolved. Current first-party position: Azure is offered, at least for Embed and BYOC. No Azure *managed* region is listed.
8. **Azure "available" vs "in production".** The same enterprise page says "BYOC in production on AWS and GCP" in its proof block and then heads a section "BYOC on AWS, GCP, and Azure (available)". **Which to model:** available on all three, in production on AWS and GCP; do not describe Azure BYOC as proven in production.

### Changed features in detail

**1. `sizes-regions` — still `partly`, four → five regions.**
E2B added **US East (AWS `us-east-1`)**, "All plans", enabled by contacting support. The full live set is US (GCP `us-west1`, default), US West (AWS `us-west-2`), US East (AWS `us-east-1`), EU (GCP `europe-west1`), APAC (GCP `asia-southeast1`). The status page independently carries `US-East-1` components, and `docs.e2b.dev/client.md` lists five domains (`e2b.app`, `foxtrot-2`, `foxtrot-3`, `e2b-juliett`, `e2b-tango`). Ceilings unchanged: 8 vCPU / 8 GiB self-serve (Pro is "8+"), 10 GiB Hobby / 20 GiB Pro disk, no GPU on any plan including BYOC. Shared prose to fix: "E2B and Blaxel list four" → five.

**2. `reaching-it` — still `partly`, but the reason changed.**
"New projects: since October 30, 2026, newly created projects default to private sandbox traffic." A private URL returns `403` without the `e2b-traffic-access-token` header, so the old figure "No auth" and the note "Public URLs are reachable by anyone who knows them" are now only true for older projects. What keeps the feature at `partly` rather than `yes`:
- the token travels in a **header**, not the URL, so a link pasted into a browser still fails without a proxy/client;
- existing projects keep public traffic unless an owner changes the project setting;
- SSH is still a WebSocket proxy (`websocat` + `openssh-server` in a custom template), not a native port;
- no raw TCP ingress is published.
Evidence: https://docs.e2b.dev/network/restrict-public-access — "A sandbox's public URL is either public (anyone with the URL can reach it) or private (every request needs the sandbox's traffic access token)." and "Requests without the `e2b-traffic-access-token` header get `403`."

**3. `built-in-agents` — still `partly`, but no longer "guides only".**
Ten named templates now install an agent: `claude` (Claude Code), `codex` (Codex), `amp` (Amp), `opencode` (OpenCode), `pi` (Pi), `prime` (Prime Agent), `grok` (Grok Build), `droid` (Factory Droid), `hermes` (Hermes Agent), `openclaw` (OpenClaw gateway). Nine pages say a variant of "E2B provides a pre-built `claude` template with Claude Code already installed." and `e2b sbx create claude` starts it; the OpenClaw page instead calls `Sandbox.create('openclaw', …)` with no build step, which only works because that template is supplied. Still `partly` because:
- the **default/base image** has no agent (unlike InstaBox or boxd);
- there is **no prompt or agent-run API** — you drive the agent through a terminal/PTY or `commands.run`;
- **model credentials are yours** (Hermes examples pass `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`; OpenClaw passes `OPENAI_API_KEY`).
Shared prose to fix: "E2B documents how to run Claude Code, Codex and Amp, but does not claim to preinstall them."

**4. `teams-accounts` — still `partly`, but SSO is no longer "not published".**
The workspace page now describes owner and member roles and project-level access lists, and says "Workspaces with SSO or SCIM have invites disabled. Members join through your identity provider instead." It also states the ceiling: "Inside a project, all members have the same permissions." There is still no SSO/SCIM setup page, protocol, plan gate or price, and no audit log. The enterprise page still calls SSO/SCIM/RBAC "planned". So: roles exist at two levels, SSO/SCIM exist but are undocumented, audit is still unpublished. Verdict stays `partly`; the old note "SSO and audit coverage are not published" is half wrong.

**5. `billing-models` — still `partly`; no money rate moved.**
Meter unchanged: allocated vCPU and RAM per running second, paused/killed free, disk included. Pro $150, Hobby $0, Pro+ $650, Pro++ $1,150 all unchanged. Two things a reader would now trip on: the **Enterprise floor is stated as $3,000 in `pricing.md` and $2,000/mo in the rendered pricing page**, and the **default sandbox RAM is stated three ways** (4 GiB pricing, 512 MiB billing FAQ, 1,024 MiB build limits). Both belong in caveats, not in the rate table. New non-money plan limits (creation rate, secrets count) are listed above.

**6. `company` — still `partly`, with one materially better fact and one worse.**
Better: SOC 2 Type II, DPA template, penetration test, bridge letter and certificate of insurance are all enumerated on `https://e2b.dev/security.md`, and the trust centre is named as authoritative. Worse: the HIPAA BAA is no longer "on request" for anyone — "E2B signs HIPAA Business Associate Agreements on Enterprise plans", so a Hobby/Pro buyer cannot get one. Status: US-West-1 shows **99.79%** over the rolling window **14 Aug – 12 Nov 2026** (the previous "99.78% July–October" was the same 90-day widget a fortnight earlier); **16 incidents** are listed in the 6 Aug – 12 Nov window (first 20 Aug, last 11 Nov), longest still 4.75 h on 15 Sep. The incidents API carries no component field, so the old "13 of them touching US-West-1" cannot be re-derived — see Open questions.

## Open questions (searched, still not published)

1. **Volume and snapshot storage price.** Volumes are separate storage objects but have no rate on the pricing page or in billing docs; snapshots likewise. `storage.disk` is "Free" only up to the included 10/20 GiB. Treat both as unknown, not zero.
2. **Egress price or allowance.** No bandwidth line on the rate card; the FAQ says traffic leaves from ephemeral IPs, not what it costs. `egress.perGiB: null` is still correct.
3. **Enterprise terms.** The rendered page gives a floor ($2,000/mo, 500 concurrent) but not the per-unit rates above it, the commit length, or whether unused commit rolls over. `pricing.md` still says $3,000.
4. **SSO/SCIM plan gate, protocol and price.** Behaviour is described for workspaces that have it; no page says how to get it, whether it is SAML or OIDC, or what it costs. The enterprise page calls it planned.
5. **Audit log.** No product audit log, retention period, export or API. BYOC gives you your cloud's audit log, which is not the same thing.
6. **Support response times.** "Discord and support@e2b.dev on every plan" and an Enterprise agreement; no published SLA tiers or response targets.
7. **Data deletion after cancellation.** The terms end access and leave fees due; no deadline for deleting sandboxes, snapshots, volumes or backups. Privacy policy lets you delete the account, not what happens to derived copies.
8. **Control-plane residency.** EU compute exists (`europe-west1`); where workspace metadata, API keys, secrets and billing data live is not stated. The EU FAQ only promises a compute cluster.
9. **Snapshot/volume ceilings.** No maximum snapshot size, count, retention or per-project volume capacity is published. The volumes beta page lists behaviour limits, not capacity limits.
10. **Nested virtualisation.** No page says whether `/dev/kvm` is exposed. Docker works; Firecracker-in-Firecracker, Android emulators and Kata are untested by the docs.
11. **Disk above 20 GiB.** "Contact support" is the whole answer: no size ladder, no price, no region constraint. Same for RAM above 8 GiB.
12. **Whether the 80% concurrency warning is UI-only.** The changelog says the usage page "warns"; no email, webhook or API event is documented.

## Bottom line

E2B still bills the same per-second rates for the same shapes, so the guide's worked costs — about $28 for the batch job, $242 of compute for the month-long job that cannot actually be bought as specified, and $165 for the interactive job including Pro — remain correct. What changed is the packaging around them: the **Enterprise floor fell by a third, from $3,000 to a $2,000/month commit with 500 concurrent sandboxes**, the **$0 Hobby plan now allows 10 sandboxes at once instead of 20**, there is a **fifth US-East region**, and **new projects get private URLs by default** behind a per-sandbox token. E2B's ready-made agent templates have grown **from one (Hermes) to ten** (Claude Code, Codex, Amp, OpenCode, Pi, Prime, Grok, Droid, Hermes, OpenClaw), which is why the "bring your own agent" rows need rewording. For the site, that means **six capability verdicts change** (`private-url` yes; `roles`, `sso`, `one-click-openclaw`, `big-machines`, `big-disk` partly) and **nine feature rows** need new figures or notes; no cost total moves, and no new test fixture is needed.
